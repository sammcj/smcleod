A template team charter for software and platform engineering teams.

Research[^1] shows there is great value in people embracing a shared vision and ideas that are bigger than themselves.

When it comes to a team charter, it's important to remember that it's not a static document. It's a living document that should be updated as the team evolves and grows.

Keep in mind that

- One size doesn't fit all.
- Consider your (teams) mission, cultural background and values.
- These can give you talking points when performing postmortems, retros and during times of conflict.

_See also: '[Using Old Ways of Thinking to Apply New Ways of Working](https://itrevolution.com/antipattern-1-2-using-old-ways-of-thinking-to-apply-new-ways-of-working/)' and '[BVSSHJ Principles](https://itrevolution.com/bvssh-principles/)'_.

With all that in mind, here's a template I use when working with teams, it is not designed to be used as-is, but rather the relevant parts should be distilled and used as a concise starting point for discussion.

## Charter

### Platform Engineering - Team Charter

This charter sets the vision, mission, values, culture and communication protocols for our Platform Engineering team.

#### Team Vision / Mission Statement

_Add these to your team's wiki landing page._

"To add value for the business, coworkers and customers by building, running and improving the (cloud) platforms and services used by engineers and applications, including infrastructure, automation, integrations and CI/CD tooling."

#### Team Values

_Pick 5-10_

We value:

- Outcomes and business value over output and technical value.
- Trust, but verify, with guard rails.
- Small, incremental, easy to review changes over large, slow moving ones.
- Good over perfect, and rework over no work.
- Simple solutions that solve for 80% over complex solutions that solve for 95%.
- Finishing work over starting work, and pulling work over pushing it.
- Automation and light touch systems over manual work and toil (work you don't want to do).
- Widely adopted tooling over bespoke development.
- Small teams of 3 to 5 people.
- Fast feedback and evidence based conclusions.
- Embracing change, with reasonably disposable solutions.
- Concise writing.
- Loose coupling and tight integration.
- Breaking down silos - there is no us and them, only us.
- Sharing knowledge in a central repository anyone in the organisation can search.
- Adopting useful practices from frameworks rather than trying to 'implement' them.
- People over tools.

#### Team Culture and Code of Conduct

_Add these to your team's wiki landing page and applicable software repositories._

_Pick 3-5_

We:

- Keep an empathetic, blameless culture of psychological safety, where people can make mistakes and be themselves without fear for their status or career.
- Respect each other's time, including working hours, timezones and work/life balance.
- Ask for help when we need it and support those who ask.
- Operate as peers and assume good intent.
- Recognise and celebrate individual and team accomplishments.
- Collaborate where possible and decide by consensus, accepting that the team lead may break ties or make final calls.
- Accept that knowledge varies across the team and each person adds value in different ways.
- Document major architectural decisions and make delegation visible.

#### Team Communication

_Add these to your team's wiki landing page._

_Pick 3-5_

We:

- Listen to understand the views and values of others.
- Work transparently so we can learn from each other.
- Agree on and document our core working and meeting hours.
- Give direct, constructive, positive feedback.
- Use principled negotiation rather than positional bargaining.
- Hold meetings only with value and an agenda, at reasonable hours, and arrive prepared and on time.
- Use asynchronous communication for messages that aren't urgent or that several people should read in their own time.
